# Ride: walking on a moving body (E12)

A train, a platform, a raft: a body that moves, with the player and the agents standing on it. Built in rounds (R1 the player, R2 agents as passengers, R3 the train node); this page says what exists.

## R1: the player is carried by the body it stands on
- **What**: when the player's feet rest on a body (a contact within 45 degrees of up, the same one that already presses the player's weight on it), the player is carried by it. At the start of each step the player goes where the point of the body it stood on went since the last step, turns with it (the body's turn about the player's up turns the player's view by the same angle), and its own move is relative to the platform. Walking is relative to the platform (a 4.2 m/s walk is 4.2 m/s over the deck), a jump keeps the platform's speed (the player comes down on the deck again), walking off keeps the speed for a moment like leaving a belt.
- **Which point**: a **kinematic** body (one the game or a train node moves) carries the exact point of it the feet stood on, so translation and rotation both count (a platform that swings round takes a player 2 m from its middle round on the circle). A loose, dynamic body (a crate, a raft) carries by its centre and its turn about up only: a rolling log or ball must not carry the foot round with its spin.
- **Tick order**: the player steps first, then the bodies move (`PhysicsWorld::step` in the tick tail, or the game for a kinematic body). The next step starts by carrying the player with that motion, before anything else of its own: a fast train does not leave the capsule behind and does not push it out sideways (the "moved out of every body it touches" rule never meets a carried player, because it is already where the deck is). A body that jumps more than 8 m in a tick is not a ride (a teleported platform).
- **Platform speed** is the displacement of the point over the tick, not the body's `vel` (a kinematic body that the game places has none): the speed is known exactly after the first tick on the deck; a player that stands still on a platform that starts moving is carried without sliding.
- **State**: the ride is not part of the replay state (a replay never steps the player: it reads the logged pose); the carried positions are logged like any step's. A teleport (`setPose` / `setState`) drops the ride. `Player::supportBody()` names the body (0: none); the game reads it through the usual body queries.
- **API**: `Player::bodyById` (a callback the app sets: `phys.find`) is what turns it on; a Player without it behaves as before.
- **Loose bodies turn the player only when it is worth it**: a dynamic body that is under 200 kg and not the game's (`owned`) carries translation only, and every dynamic body's turn is ignored below half a degree a tick: a corpse part or a loot crate under the player's weight creeps and micro-spins every tick and the view must not swim. A kinematic body keeps the exact point. A 2.9 t body that is shoved carries the player with the shove and does not turn the view with its settling.
- Tests (`tests/test_ride.cpp`): a platform at 2 m/s for 600 ticks (the player stays within a voxel of its place, bit for bit the same twice), a platform turning 172 degrees under a player 2 m from its middle (carried round, the view turned with it), walking and a jump on a moving deck, a ride that ends when the body is removed. The recorded-ride replay comes with R3's train (docs/RIDE.md will list it).

## R2: agents as passengers
```lua
agent.ride(id, body [, {x, y, z}])   -- true / false: the agent stands on `body`: the offset is where its FEET are, in metres from the body's centre of mass in the body's own frame (omit: where it stands now)
agent.unride(id)                      -- it stands where it is and falls to the floor from there
agent.info(id).riding                 -- the body (absent: none)
```
- While it rides, the agent's place is the body's pose times its offset every tick and it turns with the body (its yaw minus the body's stays). It does **not path**: `agent.walk`, `follow` and `wander` return false until `unride`. It plays no walk clip and a procedural agent's legs do not walk (the model is placed, not followed, so the layer sees no motion); a head still turns to the player. `agent.info` gives world coordinates, so the game fights, aims and hurts it in world space like any agent; its kinematic body moves with the deck (what it pushes, it pushes at the deck's speed).
- It falls off in two cases: the body is removed (it stands where it was, falls from there, and is a walking agent again) and the agent's model goes limp (a ragdoll is where physics takes it).
- **Boarding is the game's job**: at a stop of the train the game calls `agent.ride` for those on the deck and `agent.unride` for those that step off where the floor is (the train node fires `stopped` / `departing`). No navigation on a moving frame: monsters that board a moving train or fight on it while it moves are not promised.
- The ride is in the agents' state hash (the body, the offset and the yaw offset). Test `agent: eight passengers ...` (eight agents on a platform for 24 s with four turns: each stays within a voxel of its place on the deck, no `fell`, bit for bit the same twice; one steps off at a stop and walks 17 m over the voxels; the platform is removed and the rest are walking agents). The MCP smoke with an agent on the train comes with R3.

## R3: the train node, `events.train`
```lua
tr = events.train{ name = "iron_tangle",
    body  = { width = 24, thickness = 4, length = 96, material = "metal" },   -- voxels; +z is along its way; under 64 m long (length <= 1024)
    track = { {x, y, z}, {x, y, z}, ... },                                    -- voxel waypoints: where the MIDDLE OF THE DECK'S TOP goes (2 to 512)
    closed = true,           -- a loop (default); false: ridden to and fro with `end_seconds` (default 2) at each end
    speed = 4, accel = 1.5,  -- m/s and m/s^2 (stops are eased: it brakes to stand exactly on the waypoint)
    stops = { { 2, 4 }, { 4, 2 } },   -- { waypoint number (1-based), seconds }
    start_on = true }        -- false: it waits until `on`
events.train_body(tr)         -- the physics body: agent.ride(agent, events.train_body(tr)), what the player stands on
events.connect(btn, "pressed", tr, "toggle")      -- commands: on off toggle reverse start (leave the stop now) reset
events.on(tr, "arrived", function(e) ... e.stop (1-based), e.moving, e.body end)   -- signals: departing (on), stopped (off), arrived (pulse); also events.connect(tr, "arrived", other, "on")
```
- **What it is**: a kinematic body (nothing pushes it, it pushes crates and bodies and never collides with the world, so the **track's voxels are the game's decoration and the game keeps a free tunnel for it**) that the tick moves along the track: `events.tick` places it before the physics step, like an agent. It never leaves the track: a piecewise-linear path in metres from the waypoints, the body's yaw follows the segment (a radian a second at the corners), the deck stays at the waypoints' height.
- **Motion**: it accelerates to `speed`, brakes with `accel` so that it stands on the stop's waypoint (a stop of 0 seconds is a waypoint it goes through), waits the seconds (the node's level is off, signal `stopped`, then pulse `arrived`; counter = the stop's number), then sets off (`departing`). `off` brakes it to a halt where it is and holds it, `on` goes again, `reverse` brakes and turns it round, `start` ends a wait at once, `reset` puts it back at the first waypoint. Everything is a function of the tick (floats with + - * / and sqrt): two runs agree bit for bit and a replay needs nothing logged.
- **Riders**: a player standing on the deck is carried (R1), an agent given to `agent.ride` with the deck's body is carried (R2). Boarding is the game's: the node's `stopped` / `departing` events say when to call `agent.ride` / `agent.unride`.
- **State**: the place on the track (millimetres), direction, speed, wait and hold are saved with the other node states (`.events/state.txt`) and in the state hash; a declaration of the same train again keeps it where it is, another description starts it over; `remove` takes the body with it. MCP `events` lists it (`kind: train`; a train is declared from Lua, not by `event_create`).
- **Limits**: no passengers on a moving frame that board by themselves, no switching between tracks, no train over 64 m, no slopes of the deck (it keeps the waypoints' heights but follows them: a ramp track tilts nothing; the deck stays flat). The far levels draw it like any body.
- Tests: `train: ...` (a loop with two stops waits the seconds, stands on the waypoints, never goes over its speed; bit for bit the same twice; a save restores its place; off / on / start / reverse; an open track to and fro with a player carried through the stops, within 5 cm of its place on the deck over 70 s), the MCP smoke test (the player rides it, an agent rides as a passenger, the arrival reaches the script, the recording replays to its hash).
